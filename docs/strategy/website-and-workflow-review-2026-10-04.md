# Website and workflow review

Assessment: October 4, 2026, America/Los_Angeles. This extends the existing market thesis with a fresh review of official competitor pages and the current Vraelis site. It does not establish customer demand or competitor product performance.

## What the research changes

Foxglove publicly describes robot recording comparison, live connections, data management and agent-assisted debugging. Its defense page names the industry, describes synchronized logs and sensor visualization, then shows customer examples. A prettier map or a general robot-log assistant would encounter direct competition.

Formant's Analytics documentation includes streams, events and task summaries that can combine multiple streams. The relevant baseline is therefore not a dashboard that cannot understand tasks. A pilot must compare Vraelis against the customer's strongest existing investigation or scripted review.

Palantir action test runs simulate an action against supplied parameters and expose proposed Ontology edits and execution details. Those edits are not committed to the Ontology, while some function-backed external calls can execute. Vraelis should not suggest that an Ontology cannot support testing or that all its tests are mocked.

The viable hypothesis remains a focused repeatable review: the agreed task, intended asset, deadline, source reports and missing observations. We have not proven that competitors cannot implement it. We need evidence that an integrator or engineering team finds the current alternative costly enough to pay for a better workflow.

## Website changes made

The approved cinematic opening and centered tagline remain. A short product line now names control software, device reports and the three focus areas. The recording example appears before the older browser demonstration. The interactive sample uses the existing evaluator on explicitly simulated recordings and exposes failure, insufficient evidence and a corrected result.

Defense, Robotics, Infrastructure and Platform now describe recorded reports and browser execution as separate workflows. The browser demonstration does not claim to observe a physical device. The new public recording guide lists exact supported formats and limits, reviewed criteria, comparison, export and the absence of cloud persistence. Social descriptions now reflect physical systems rather than a retired web-app-only pitch.

The Defense FAQ previously included a specific government supplier restriction with no cited current primary source. That claim has been removed rather than repeated as established law. The replacement states actual product deployment limits and asks prospective institutions to assess their own supplier requirements. This does not conclude anything about a particular government contract.

## Next product work worth validating

1. Obtain an authorized example from a robotics integrator or device-software team: one task, a known disagreement, separate source reports and their current investigation method. This requires the user's future participation; no outreach was sent.
2. Confirm that task and asset identity, clocks and captured intervals can be reconstructed reliably. Two reports copied from one controller do not establish independent observations. If the evidence cannot support the question, identify that before developing an adapter.
3. Select the first adapter from that customer's actual format. Today the beta supports normalized JSON and a narrow uncompressed MCAP JSON subset. It does not support arbitrary ROS bags, CDR, compressed MCAP, live hardware or restricted-environment deployment.
4. Repeat the same reviewed criteria on a changed build. Measure investigation time, reviewer effort, incorrect conclusions, missing-evidence handling and the effort to maintain the mapping. Compare against their strongest incumbent workflow or script.
5. Ask whether the same reviewer needs this again and what approval artifact they require. Do not build a general operations platform, ontology replacement or sensor-ground-truth engine without a demonstrated workflow need.

A commercial win would be repeat useful findings and lower review effort on customer evidence. A simulation, a source hash and an attractive UI do not establish a defensible business by themselves. Retained mappings, reviewed task definitions and repeatable comparisons could make the workflow valuable, but only with adequate operational evidence and actual adoption.
